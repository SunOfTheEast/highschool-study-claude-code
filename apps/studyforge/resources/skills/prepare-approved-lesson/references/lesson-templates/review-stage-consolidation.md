# Stage-consolidation review Lesson

## Use when

Use this when recent Lessons have produced useful but scattered methods, examples,
or distinctions that must become one usable structure inside the current Plan.

## Intended change

Turn recent experience into organised route knowledge: what a method is for, how it
differs from its neighbours, and how to choose it on a new task.

## Necessary teaching functions

- Reread the relevant closed Lessons and retrieve representative ideas before recap.
- Ask the student to compare adjacent methods, conditions, and failure boundaries.
- Reorganise fragments into a small usable structure rather than a long summary.
- Finish with route choice, mixed use, or transfer that requires the structure.

## Default activity path and depth

Use retrieval from recent work, method grouping, one or two sharp contrasts, a mixed
selection task, and a transfer or stage check. Reuse original material only when the
goal is reconstruction; use new material for evidence of use. Activity count is
capacity, not a quota.

## Adaptation from student evidence

Compress what the student already organises well. Spend depth on confused boundaries
or misleading similarities. If retrieval is unexpectedly weak, teach locally rather
than continuing an artificial cumulative test.

## First-pass information boundary

Method names and the review map may be visible because organisation is the teaching
goal. Preserve the student's route choice on new tasks by withholding decisive
mapping cues and answers.

## Sufficient closing evidence

The student can reconstruct the important structure and use it to choose or transfer
on representative material. Do not claim delayed retention from same-stage review.

## Reflection default

Ask for a compact personal map, contrast, or route-selection rule when it will be
useful in later Lessons. Avoid a chronological retelling of everything taught.

Do not turn this default into fixed minutes, fixed Block IDs, runtime fields, or a
template-specific schema.
