# Systematic-review cycle

## Use when

Use this when review, reorganisation, and cumulative use are the Plan's main progress
objective—not merely because an older item has become due for retrieval.

## Stage problem

The student must recover representative knowledge, rebuild relations and boundaries,
and actively select and transfer it in mixed work.

## Minimum starting information

Know the review boundary, the Plan standard and direct check, the relevant earlier
Plans or Lessons, and any time-chain candidates placed on the Coach's desk. A due
candidate is an option, not proof of forgetting or a scheduling command.

## Default phase graph

```text
cold representative retrieval
→ rebuild structure
→ compare confusable boundaries
→ mixed active use
→ local repair
→ cumulative transfer and stage check
```

## Evidence sought at each phase

- Retrieval: what is independently available after delay.
- Structure: whether ideas and methods can be related rather than listed.
- Boundaries: whether nearby cases are discriminated.
- Mixed use: whether the student actively selects what applies.
- Repair: whether observed weak points respond to targeted teaching.
- Transfer and check: whether the Plan's review standard is exercised cumulatively.

## Branches and loops

Compress strong material and spend depth on actual weak points. Repair isolated gaps
locally rather than restarting the whole topic. Add comparison when the method map is
fragmented. If broad foundations are missing, return to Roadmap and define a different
stage problem.

## Sufficient completion boundary

The student retrieves a representative core, reconstructs useful structure, and
independently selects or transfers it within the declared review boundary. Immediate
post-reteaching success is not delayed retention.

## Common Lesson templates

Spaced-retrieval review, stage-consolidation review, error remediation, deliberate
practice, and capability assessment as the evidence requires.

## Common misjudgment

Do not replay chapters evenly, force every review candidate into the Plan, or confuse
same-session relearning with retained access.

A phase may occupy zero, one, or several Lessons and may be skipped, repeated,
merged, or revisited. Do not encode fixed Lesson IDs, counts, minutes, mastery scores,
or runtime phase fields.
