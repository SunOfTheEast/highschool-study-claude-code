# Diagnostic cycle

## Use when

Use this when competing explanations for the student's difficulty would lead to
materially different Plans and current evidence cannot distinguish them responsibly.

## Stage problem

The goal is to reduce decision-changing uncertainty, not to master a method during
the same cycle or produce a flattering student profile.

## Minimum starting information

Name the few plausible explanations, why each would change the next stage, what the
student currently believes, and what independent observations could distinguish the
alternatives.

## Default phase graph

```text
name competing explanations
→ complementary short probes
→ observe starts, stops, and help
→ discriminating recheck
→ student confirmation
→ return to Roadmap for the next substantive Plan
```

## Evidence sought at each phase

- Alternatives: whether the uncertainty is concrete enough to test.
- Probes: which explanation fits independent starts and stopping points.
- Help observation: what kind of support changes the work.
- Recheck: whether the key distinction repeats on another probe.
- Confirmation: whether the student's account changes the interpretation.

## Branches and loops

End early when the alternatives are clearly separated. Add one targeted probe when
results conflict. If the student requests teaching, end that item's independent
status and preserve the boundary. If material, fatigue, emotion, or wording explains
the result, do not relabel it as mathematical incapacity.

## Sufficient completion boundary

The resulting evidence rules out important wrong directions and is sufficient for
Roadmap and student to define the next substantive Plan. Completion does not require
mastery of a content method.

## Common Lesson templates

Starting-point diagnostic; short probes may also occur in Plan conversation when no
full Lesson is needed.

## Common misjudgment

Do not write “Coach now understands the student” as the observable standard, or let
one long problem confound route choice, execution, endurance, and help dependence.

A phase may occupy zero, one, or several Lessons and may be skipped, repeated,
merged, or revisited. Do not encode fixed Lesson IDs, counts, minutes, mastery scores,
or runtime phase fields.
