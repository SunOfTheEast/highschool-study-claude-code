# Deliberate-practice Lesson

## Use when

Use this when a known method needs greater stability, discrimination, route choice,
or transfer across related structures.

## Intended change

Improve a named decision or performance, not merely accumulate completed problems.
The student should see what stays invariant, what changes, and when a route is worth
using.

## Necessary teaching functions

- Re-establish the target decision with a short entry task.
- Give at least one problem enough depth for a complete independent solution.
- Contrast nearby structures or routes rather than presenting a list of tricks.
- Vary one meaningful feature at a time before asking for unfamiliar transfer.
- End with a brief task that actually exercises the stated target.

## Default activity path and depth

Normally prepare 4–6 problem materials with different jobs: entry recognition, a
core baseline, a contrast, a changed shell, transfer, and an exit check. A common
one-hour capacity is about two full solutions plus several oral, comparison, or
decisive-step tasks. The count is not a quota, and no single “perfect problem” must
carry every teaching function.

## Adaptation from student evidence

Skip redundant entry work, deepen a useful comparison, or activate a prepared
reserve only when a named student response calls for it. Repeated execution errors
may justify a narrower corrective Block; route confusion may require comparison
before another full solution.

## First-pass information boundary

The topic, method names, source, workload, and activity shape may be public. Preserve
the student's decisions by withholding the unseen problem's decisive transformation,
answer, expected trap, and preferred route until the interaction calls for them.

## Sufficient closing evidence

Close when the student has independently exercised the target on representative new
material and the Lesson log makes remaining limits visible. Fluency on repeated or
heavily supported examples is not yet transfer.

## Reflection default

Prefer a compact comparison—what cue mattered, why one route was cheaper, or what
would trigger a switch—when that improves the next attempt. Omit ceremonial recap.

Do not turn this default into fixed minutes, fixed Block IDs, runtime fields, or a
template-specific schema.
